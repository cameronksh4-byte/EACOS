# 1. The In-Person Walk-In Playbook

The walk-in isn't a sales call. It's a **neighbor stopping by with one sharp question**. The only goal of a first visit is one of these outcomes, in order of preference:

1. **Pilot started today** (10-minute setup on the spot)
2. **Pilot booked**, with a date, a time, and the clerk's name
3. **Second visit booked** with the Owner or LCB
4. **WhatsApp number plus permission to follow up**, which starts the [follow-up sequence](04-follow-up-sequences.md)

Anything else is just a visit. Log it and move on.

---

## 1.1 When to walk in

Brokerages and cold-storage 3PLs run on the bridge's rhythm. Walk in at the wrong hour and you're the guy who interrupted a hold. Walk in at the right hour and you're the only person all day who had time to listen.

| Time | Customs brokerage | Cold-storage 3PL / yard | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6:00–10:00** | Morning bridge rush: releases, holds, manifest problems, phones ringing | Overnight loads moving, outbound dispatch | ❌ **Never walk in** |
| **10:00–11:45** | First real lull. The morning wave is released and the owner is back at a desk. | Mid-morning lull | ✅ **Prime window A, the best of the day** |
| **11:45–1:15** | Lunch. Owners are out. | Lunch | ⚠️ Only to drop a one-pager with the front desk and ask when the quiet hour is |
| **1:15–3:00** | Second lull, good for sit-down time with the Owner or LCB | Ramping up for afternoon and evening northbound receiving | ✅ **Prime window B for brokerages.** Skip 3PLs after 2:00. |
| **3:00–close** | Afternoon and evening crossing push (produce especially) | Peak receiving | ❌ **Never walk in** |

**Day of week:** Tuesday through Thursday. Mondays are weekend backlog. Friday afternoons are the push to cross before the weekend.

**Season:** we're heading into the winter produce ramp (Nov–Jan). Volume rises, and so does the value of every catch, but so does stress. Keep visits short and respect "come back after season" (log the date and honor it).

**Validate in Week 1.** These windows are a starting hypothesis. At every front desk, ask: *"¿A qué hora está más tranquilo aquí?"* ("When is it quietest here?") Log the answer in the CRM as `quiet_hour`. By Week 3 you'll have each office's real rhythm.

## 1.2 Route planning

- **Cluster 4–6 stops within about 2 miles** so a 90-minute prime window covers 3–4 real visits.
- **Pharr corridor (Tue/Wed):** S. Jackson Rd, Cage Blvd, Military Hwy and the streets around the Pharr–Reynosa bridge approach. Add Hidalgo/McAllen brokerages as a secondary loop.
- **Brownsville corridor (Thursday):** Port of Brownsville (Hwy 48 corridor), the Veterans International Bridge (Los Tomates) area, and the Free Trade Bridge (Los Indios) area.
- **Order each loop A → B → A:** a Tier A brokerage first while you're fresh, a Tier B 3PL in the middle, then a Tier A brokerage again.
- **Second visits** go in the same windows. Book them by name through the front desk: *"Can I come back Wednesday at 10:30 to see [Owner] for 10 minutes?"*

## 1.3 How to show up

- **Dress:** clean jeans or chinos, a polo or button-down, comfortable boots or work shoes. Look like you've stood in a yard before. No suit, no startup hoodie.
- **Language:** open in whichever language they greet you in. Spanglish is fine. Match them.
- **Carry:** one-pagers in a clean folder, the phone with the demo loaded, one pre-provisioned pilot kit, a notebook. That's it. No laptop bag.
- **Time on site:** 7 minutes if it's a no, 20 if it's a pilot. Never overstay.

## 1.4 At the front desk (gatekeeper)

Respect the front desk, because they decide whether you ever see the owner. Learn their name and write it down.

**English**
> "Hi, good morning, I'm [Rep Name]. I'm local, from [Weslaco]. I'm not here to sell anything today. I just have one question for whoever catches paperwork mistakes before the trucks cross. Is [Ops Manager / Owner] around for two minutes? If now's a bad time, when is it usually quietest?"

**Español**
> "Buenos días, soy [Nombre], soy de aquí, de [Weslaco]. No vengo a venderle nada hoy. Nada más tengo una pregunta para la persona que revisa que los papeles estén bien antes de que crucen los camiones. ¿Estará [Gerente / Dueño] dos minutos? Si ahorita no es buen momento, ¿a qué hora está más tranquilo?"

**If the decision maker isn't there:** hand over the one-pager in its folder with a **handwritten sticky note**: *"[Owner name], one question about pre-bridge paperwork. I'll come back [Wed 10:30]. [Rep], [WhatsApp number]."* Ask the front desk for the owner's or ops manager's WhatsApp, or at least their own name, so you can ask for that person next time.

## 1.5 The 60-second pitch

The pitch rests on one real question and a pause. Let them say the clerk's name. Once they do, they've made the problem concrete without you having to.

### English (Owner, LCB or Ops Manager)

> "Thanks for the minute. I know the trucks don't wait. I'm [Rep Name], I'm from here.
>
> I'm not here to sell you a new system. I've got one question. When a factura comes in on WhatsApp at 6:40 in the morning saying **22 pallets, 18,200 kilos**, and the BOL says **20 pallets, 36,000 pounds**, who catches that before the entry goes in?
>
> *[Pause. Let them answer. It's usually a name: "Lupita."]*
>
> Right. Lupita's your safety net. We built a second set of eyes for her. Your people keep doing exactly what they do now. The facturas, the Carta Porte and the BOL come in by WhatsApp or email, and they just forward them to us too. In about 12 seconds we line them up, convert kilos and pounds, back out the pallet tare, and check the box counts. If something doesn't match, your ops manager and the yard get a message **before anything goes to ACE**.
>
> There's nothing to install, and it doesn't touch CargoWise or your ABI. The data stays on U.S. servers. And we're software only: we don't broker, we don't forward, and we will never call your clients.
>
> Here's what I'd ask. Let us shadow you for 14 days. If we don't catch at least three real paperwork errors, you owe nothing. Can I show you one on my phone? It takes two minutes."

### Español

> "Gracias por el minuto, sé que los camiones no esperan. Soy [Nombre], soy de aquí.
>
> No vengo a venderle un sistema nuevo. Nada más tengo una pregunta: cuando llega una factura por WhatsApp a las 6:40 de la mañana que dice **22 tarimas, 18,200 kilos**, y el BOL dice **20 tarimas, 36,000 libras**, ¿quién lo detecta antes de que se transmita la entrada?
>
> *[Pausa. Deje que contesten. Casi siempre dicen un nombre: "Lupita."]*
>
> Exacto. Lupita es su red de seguridad. Nosotros hicimos un segundo par de ojos para ella. Su gente sigue trabajando igual: las facturas, la Carta Porte y el BOL llegan por WhatsApp o correo, y nada más nos los reenvían a nosotros también. En unos 12 segundos los comparamos: convertimos kilos y libras, descontamos el peso de las tarimas y contamos las cajas. Si algo no cuadra, su gerente de operaciones y el patio reciben un mensaje **antes de que se transmita a ACE**.
>
> No hay que instalar nada. No toca CargoWise ni su sistema ABI. Los datos se quedan en servidores en Estados Unidos. Y somos solo software: no somos agencia aduanal, no somos forwarders, y nunca vamos a contactar a sus clientes.
>
> Lo que le propongo: déjenos acompañarlos 14 días en modo sombra. Si no encontramos por lo menos tres errores reales en la documentación, no nos debe nada. ¿Le enseño uno en mi teléfono? Son dos minutos."

### Adjust the emphasis by who you're talking to

| Talking to | Lean into | One line to add |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner / LCB** | License, clients, margin | "Every catch is timestamped, so you also get a written record of the diligence your team already does." |
| **Operations Manager** | Fewer fires, trucks moving | "The alert goes to you and to the yard, so you hear about it before the driver's in line, not after." |
| **Lead clerk** (if she's in the room) | Not being replaced, getting credit | "It doesn't decide anything. It hands you the two numbers that don't match, and you fix it." |
| **Cold-storage 3PL** | Detention, dock doors, reefer fuel | "How long does a reefer sit at your door when the paperwork's wrong? We tell your yard before it pulls in." |

### Words to never use

"AI-powered platform," "disrupt," "leverage," "synergy," "end-to-end," "seamless," "revolutionize," "guaranteed compliance," "replace your clerks," and any competitor's name in a negative sentence. Say "it checks," "it catches," "it tells you," "before the bridge."

## 1.6 Discovery: 5 questions, then shut up

Ask these if they give you more than two minutes. Write the answers down **in their words**, because you'll read them back on Day 14.

1. "How do the facturas and BOLs usually get to you: WhatsApp, email, both? From the agencia, the carrier, the shipper?"
2. "On a heavy day, how many entries do you file?"
3. "When was the last time paperwork cost you, whether it was a truck sent back, a hold, a correction, or a client upset?" *(Let them tell the whole story.)*
4. "What did that one cost you, roughly, in detention, re-dray and people's time?"
5. "If something caught that before it happened, who here would you want to get the alert?"

## 1.7 The 2-minute phone demo

1. Show a **real, sanitized** WhatsApp chat: a factura photo and a BOL PDF forwarded in.
2. About 12 seconds later, show the **alert card** with both document crops side by side and the mismatched field highlighted:
   `22 tarimas · 18,200 kg (factura)` vs `20 pallets · 36,000 lb = 16,329 kg (BOL)`, giving **pallets −2, weight −1,871 kg (−10%)**.
3. Show a **clean** load too: "✅ Matched: factura, Carta Porte and BOL agree." Seeing a pass makes them believe the fail.
4. Close the demo with the ask: *"That's it. Want to try it on your own loads for 14 days?"*

## 1.8 Asking for the pilot

Close for the **pilot**, never for the software.

> "Here's what it takes: about 10 minutes with whoever handles the intake, today or any morning this week around 10:30. Nothing gets installed, and nobody changes how they work. On Day 14 I come back with a list of every error we caught. If there aren't at least three real ones, you owe nothing and you keep the list."

If yes, start the [10-minute setup](03-shadow-pilot-blueprint.md#32-the-10-minute-zero-it-setup) right now, or book it with a date, time and the clerk's name.

## 1.9 Objection-handling matrix

Every objection gets four moves: **hear what they actually mean → answer it straight → prove it → move one step forward.** Never argue. Agree with what's true in the objection first.

### "We use CargoWise." (or NetCHB, Descartes, Magaya)

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they mean** | "We already paid for a system. I don't want another one, and I don't want IT involved." |
| **Say** | "Good. Keep it. CargoWise is where the entry gets built. We work **before** that, in the WhatsApp chat and the inbox, where the factura and the BOL show up as a photo or a PDF and nobody's compared them yet. CargoWise checks that what your clerk typed is valid. It doesn't know the Carta Porte said 22 pallets when the BOL said 20. We hand your clerk a matched set *before* she keys it. And nothing connects to CargoWise; we never touch it." |
| **Español** | "Perfecto, quédese con él. CargoWise es donde se arma la entrada. Nosotros trabajamos **antes**, en el WhatsApp y en el correo, donde llegan la factura y el BOL como foto o PDF y nadie los ha comparado todavía. CargoWise revisa que lo que se capturó sea válido, pero no sabe que la Carta Porte decía 22 tarimas y el BOL 20. Y no nos conectamos a CargoWise para nada." |
| **Prove it** | Show the alert card. Both source documents are cited, and nothing came from CargoWise. |
| **Move forward** | "Which inbox does the Reynosa paperwork land in? Let's forward just that one for 14 days." |

### "Our clerks check things manually."

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they mean** | "My people are good. Are you saying they're not?" This is pride and loyalty talking, so **never contradict it.** |
| **Say** | "They should, and they'll keep doing it. I'm not here to replace anybody. How many does she check on a heavy day? … At 7 in the morning with 40 trucks in line, even the best clerk misses one now and then. That's not a people problem, it's a volume problem. We're the second set of eyes, so she spends her time fixing problems instead of doing kilo-to-pound math. And when we catch something, she's the one who fixes it and she gets the credit. Either way you win. If we catch errors, you avoid the cost. If we don't, you've got 14 days of proof that your team's process works, which is worth having in writing." |
| **Español** | "Y así debe ser, y lo van a seguir haciendo. No vengo a reemplazar a nadie. A las 7 de la mañana con 40 camiones en fila, hasta la mejor se le pasa uno. No es problema de la gente, es problema de volumen. Nosotros somos el segundo par de ojos. Y si encontramos algo, ella lo corrige y el crédito es de ella." |
| **Prove it** | Ask: "When was the last correction, PSC, or truck sent back over paperwork?" Let them tell the story. That story is your proof. |
| **Move forward** | "Let's put her process on paper. Fourteen days, and if we don't find three real ones, you owe nothing." |

### "Is this legal for CBP?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they mean** | "Am I putting my license at risk by sending my clients' documents to you?" This is the most important question a licensed broker can ask, so **thank them for it.** |
| **Say** | "That's exactly the right question, and I'd rather you ask it now. Three things. **One:** we never file, never transmit to ACE, and never sign anything. You still exercise responsible supervision and control; we're a review tool, like a calculator or a second clerk. **Two:** your client records are confidential under 111.24, so before you send us a single document we sign a confidentiality and service-provider agreement. We process your documents for you, only to check your shipments, and never for anything else. **Three:** it's all hosted in the U.S., the AI vendors we use keep nothing, and your records are kept with a 5-year audit trail you can export or delete. I'll leave you our one-page compliance memo. Please have your attorney look at it. We want you sure, not just convinced." |
| **Español** | "Es exactamente la pregunta correcta. Primero: nunca transmitimos a ACE, nunca firmamos nada; usted sigue teniendo la supervisión y el control. Segundo: antes de que nos mande un solo documento, firmamos un acuerdo de confidencialidad; sus documentos solo se usan para revisar sus embarques. Tercero: todo se queda en servidores en Estados Unidos, con un registro de 5 años que usted puede exportar. Le dejo el memo de cumplimiento para que lo revise su abogado." |
| **Prove it** | The compliance memo plus the signed agreement template. **Never give legal advice and never say "guaranteed compliant."** |
| **Move forward** | "Who would you want to review it? I can send it to them today and come back Thursday." |

### "We don't trust AI."

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they mean** | "AI makes things up, and I'm the one who pays the penalty." They're right to be careful. |
| **Say** | "Honestly, you shouldn't trust it blindly, and you don't have to. The AI does one job: it **reads** the PDF or the photo. The checks themselves are plain math, like kilos to pounds, pallets times tare, and box counts. It's the same math your clerk does, done the same way every time. And it never decides anything. Every alert shows both documents side by side with the number highlighted, so your clerk sees in two seconds whether it's real. For the first **three days of the pilot it stays completely silent.** It just keeps a log, and we go through it with you. You'll see exactly how often it's right before it ever messages anybody." |
| **Español** | "Tiene razón en no confiar a ciegas, y no tiene que hacerlo. La inteligencia artificial solo **lee** el documento. Las revisiones son matemáticas: kilos a libras, tarimas por peso, cajas. Y no decide nada: cada alerta le enseña los dos documentos con el número marcado. Los primeros tres días ni siquiera manda mensajes, nada más lleva un registro y lo revisamos juntos." |
| **Prove it** | The alert card with source crops, plus the ledger's **false-alarm column**. We show our misses. |
| **Move forward** | "Give us three silent days. On Day 4, you decide whether it gets to speak." |

### Other objections you'll hear

| Objection | Say this | Move forward |
|---|---|---|
| **"Send me some info."** | "Of course. So I send the right thing, what's the one paperwork problem that costs you the most?" | Get the WhatsApp number and permission, then start the [follow-up sequence](04-follow-up-sequences.md). |
| **"We're slammed, it's season."** | "That's exactly when mistakes slip through. Setup is 10 minutes at your quietest hour and nobody changes how they work. If now really isn't the time, tell me when and I'll come back then." | Book the pilot at their quiet hour, or log an "after season" date and honor it. |
| **"How much?"** | "If we don't catch three real errors in 14 days, nothing. If we do, founding partners pay [$1,250] a month, locked for a year, cancel anytime. That's usually less than one truck sent back. But let's let your own numbers decide on Day 14." | Back to the pilot. |
| **"Our agencia in Reynosa already checks it."** | "Good. Then the errors we find are the ones that slip between two good teams. That's exactly where mismatches live, between the Mexican paperwork and the U.S. entry." | "Let's include the agencia's emails in the pilot." |
| **"Will you sell to Nuvocargo or become a forwarder?"** | "No. We're software, only software. We'll never hold a brokerage license, forward freight, or contact your clients, and that's written into the contract, not just a promise." | Show the Broker-Neutral Pledge clause. |
| **"WhatsApp isn't official / isn't secure."** | "That's fair. Your team already gets the documents on WhatsApp; we just make sure they get checked. If you'd rather, we can run the whole pilot on email only." | Email-only pilot. |
| **"You're a new company. What if you disappear?"** | "Fair. It's month-to-month, you can export everything any time, and I live [twenty minutes] from here. You know where to find me." | Back to the pilot. |
| **"My clients have to approve this."** | "Understood. Our service-provider agreement is built for that. Want me to draft a short note you can send your top clients?" | Draft the client note. |

## 1.10 After every visit (within 15 minutes, in the car)

Log in the CRM:

- Company, address, corridor, tier (A/B/C)
- Who you met (name, role) and the **front desk's name**
- `quiet_hour` (what the front desk said)
- How documents arrive (WhatsApp / email / both; agencia / carrier / shipper)
- Entries per day (their estimate)
- **Their pain story, in their words**
- Software they use (CargoWise / NetCHB / Descartes / Magaya / other)
- Objections raised
- Outcome: pilot started / pilot booked (date) / second visit (date) / follow-up sequence / no
- Next action and date
